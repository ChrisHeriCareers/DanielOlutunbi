Written like a duty list. Not like the career it actually was.

"Responsible for managing engineering staff and departmental budget."

Why this happens

Most people are trained to describe their job, not their career. Somewhere between the work and the document, ownership quietly turns into duty. "Led" becomes "supported," whether that is a first team lead role or a global division. A number that would close a room never makes it onto the page. None of this is a writing problem. It is a translation problem, and it is fixable at any stage.

Before and after

Same career, any stage. Full paragraph, not just a sentence.

Entry Level

Assisted with customer inquiries and daily reporting tasks. Handled scheduling and supported the team with various administrative duties as needed.

Resolved an average of 40 customer inquiries a day, more than any other team member, and built the daily reporting template the team still uses. Took over scheduling for a six person team within two months of starting, without being asked.

Mid-Career

Responsible for plant operations across multiple sites. Worked with cross functional teams to help support production targets and quality standards.

Ran manufacturing operations across five plants in four countries, each with its own regulatory environment and labor market. Rebuilt the quality reporting process after a compliance gap was flagged, cutting audit findings in half within a year.

Executive

Contributed to product development and the technology roadmap. Worked closely with engineering and leadership to help guide strategic priorities.

Directed the technology roadmap for the product line carrying the business, over 60 percent of group revenue. Took the roadmap from an internal planning document to the reference point the board used to evaluate every major investment decision for three years.

How I work

Nothing goes in that will not hold up

Every draft starts from what you actually did, not from a template of what the role is supposed to sound like. If a claim does not hold up under its own math, I will say so and ask for the real number rather than let it slide through. If two roles on your timeline overlap in a way a reader would question, I will ask before a recruiter does. The result reads like you, at your most precise, not like a rewritten version of someone else's CV with your name on it.

A note on discretion
Career conversations are rarely simple. Most of this work happens while people are still employed, still deciding, still figuring out what they actually want next. Everything discussed stays between us.
